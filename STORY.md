# The county

The county is a walled valley with a road cut through the middle of it and a race
on that road every Sunday, and the wall is the reason the county is a county
rather than a stretch of country: nothing that lives here can get out and try its
luck somewhere easier, so the ladder in the wall's shadow has been the whole of
the sport since before anyone thought to write down the rules. The green half is
hedgerow and meadow, standing water in the low ground at Lily Deep, and a crag on
the north side that goes up four metres of bare face and puts a road on the top of
it because the road was there first and the crag came later. The other half is the
ashlands, which is not a second county but a quarter of the same one with the
green gone out of it: grey turf, basalt for a cliff, black water where the lilies
used to be, and four courses that are the temperate four with the meadow swapped
for the dead country and nothing else altered. Nobody races there on a Sunday. The
ashlands round is a swap, and the swap goes both ways.

## The racing

Every snail in the county carries five numbers, and a course asks about exactly
one of them: running, power, swimming, flying, and stamina, which is the odd one
out because stamina is not a course anybody can name on a card — it is the bar the
Grand Marathon drains out of you over two hundred and eleven metres of everything
at once. So a season is four races that each ask their own question and one that
asks all five and take the answer away from you, and a cup is that whole shape
again one rung up: the Sunday Cup for snails nobody has heard of, the Snail Open,
the Invitational, and the Grand Prix, where every snail in the field rates in three
figures and the courses run at more than twice the length they ran at the bottom,
which is why the top of the ladder is a longer walk and not merely a faster snail.

A minute of racing is an hour of the day, and the course clock says so out loud:
the Sunday Cup is raced in the morning with the sun up and the grass wet, and the
Grand Marathon closes the season in the evening light, and the whole county's
weather changes with the stopwatch.

## The ladder

A rating is a curve over the animal rather than a running average of what it has
won, so the best snail in the county rates 666 and a fresh one rates 10 and the
two numbers are drawn off the same five attributes. The ladder counts sixty-five:
sixty-four rivals and you, one number each, one more than how many of the others
rate above you. Nine of those sixty-four are club snails and they are the same
modest snail — three of everything — so they sit on exactly 175 and share one
number between them, which is the only sharing the county allows. Win a race and
the rating moves by what place you finished in, scaled by what the cup pays, so a
Grand Prix win is worth sixty rating and a Sunday Cup win is worth thirty; the
ladder takes five of those to go from 10 to 160, which is exactly where the Open's
door is, and three clean seasons to reach the top rung with nothing above it.

They are not in it.

## The Adversary

The Adversary is not a rival and never was one. They are built off the strongest
snail in the pool rather than counted in it, so they hold one rating above whoever
is currently best and move up the ladder as the ladder moves — a season ago the
top of the county rated 120 and they were out of reach entirely, and today the top
of the county rates 666 and they are one clean season above it. The ladder prints
their row and marks it and does not count them, because the ladder's own
arithmetic counts the pool, and putting them inside it would owe every snail on
the wall an answer about a snail that has no list.

They are strong and they are not quick. They carry the best snail's five numbers
with two more running than anybody else in the county has, their pace is mid-pack,
and they never surge once — they spend no stamina at all, so a duel against them
is a race decided by the road and not by a number. They wait for you at the two
ends of The Adversary's Ground, out in the ashlands, in the evening, on a
two-snail road with two crates and a countdown three seconds longer than the one
you have been hearing all year. A win is worth sixty rating, which is a season's
worth in one race, and a loss costs you the place: one below the best snail in
the county if you were above them, and exactly what you had if you were not,
because there was no place of yours to lose.

They are also the only snail in the county wearing a face and a hat that cannot be
bought. There is no price on either and no shelf to take them off, and no rule in
the wardrobe hands them out — beat The Adversary and both are yours, and they are
the only two things here that no purse ever bought.
