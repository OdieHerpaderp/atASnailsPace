meshes/hats/….glb

Seven hats and a null, one file each, all built in head-local space: the origin is the centre of a snail's head, +x forward out of the face, +y up. That is the frame headG is at in meshes/snail.glb, so a hat drops onto it at the origin and there is no offset table to keep in step with one.

The head is not a sphere with a hat on it. It is an ellipsoid of radius 0.112 scaled 1.00, 0.97, 1.02, a snail's head is four centimetres across, and the two feelers come out of it at headG + (0.045, 0.075, ±0.036) and stand 78 mm above its centre. So **every hat is authored with its own offset and none of them share one**: a straw hat sits on the crown at y = +0.070, a bonnet sits back on the shell at y = -0.010 and behind the head at x = -0.030, and the sunglasses sit on the eye line at y = -0.018 — which is why a hat file has to be written against the head it goes on and not against the last hat in the set. `headTop()` below is the crown's height at a width, and it is the only reason the hats can be written from one origin and still all sit right.

Seven files and eight names. `none` is a sentinel with no file behind it: it means no mesh at all, and the game's `hatFor('none')` answers `null` for it. A hat that does not exist and a hat that is `undefined` are the same picture, which is why the plan of the set is in HAT_SET in src/core.js and the file list is in MESH_SETS in src/materials.js, and tools/plan-test.mjs --faces is what says the two agree.

One hat is one mesh named hat-<name>, except the propeller, which is two: hat-propeller is the cap and hat-propeller-rotor is what turns. That is the loader's own part convention — a piece of two surfaces is <file> and <file>-<part> — and it is the only hat in the county that moves.

Colour is out of the county's jar (meshes/palette.js) and nothing is invented here. There is no uv on any of it: a hat is a silhouette, and a tiling map on a two-millimetre hat band is a normal map on four triangles.
