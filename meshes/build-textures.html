meshes/tex/….png

Every map the library uses, drawn on a canvas and written into meshes/tex/. The maps are not embedded in the glbs: the game loads them by name, out of the one manifest in meshes/maps.js, and a prop whose maps have not been written yet simply has none and draws as it always did. That is the whole migration strategy - one prop at a time, and the game is never broken in between.

Open this page from the folder that serves the game, press a button, and the PNG lands in meshes/tex/. Every map is seamless: the noise is sampled on a lattice that wraps at the tile, and the normals are built from a height field that wraps the same way, so a map tiled a hundred times across a course has no join in it.

Naming is the contract. The suffix says which slot the file goes in: -albedo is base colour and is read as sRGB, -n and -n2 are tangent-space normals, -rgh is roughness in green and metalness in blue, -em is emissive. The rest of the name is the prop, and a prop may share a map with its neighbours - rock-n is the grain of every stone in the county.

Albedo is full colour only if nothing recolours the prop at runtime. A prop the game tints - the snail, the three shells, the flowers - carries a low-chroma or grey map, because material.color and an instanceColor are the hue and a coloured map underneath turns a tint into a hue shift.
